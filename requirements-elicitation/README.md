# Requirements Elicitation: Evidence Engine

**Product:** an AI agent that searches GMU's library databases for scholarly
sources and quotes that support a student's thesis.

This folder holds the material we uploaded to elicit requirements: what users
say, how they work today, and what the product might look like.

| Folder | Contents |
|--------|----------|
| [`notes/`](notes/website-comments.md) | **Notes:** comments from websites. These include student and librarian comments and studies on database searching (A1–A10), and user reviews of similar AI tools such as Elicit and Consensus (B1–B10). |
| [`recordings/`](recordings/youtube-videos.md) | **Recordings:** YouTube videos of people using GMU Mason Search and similar apps such as Elicit, Consensus, Scite, and Google Scholar (R1–R15), with observed friction. |
| [`images/`](images/README.md) | **Images:** a diagram of the current workflow and wireframes of the planned product, plus a folder for screenshots of existing tools. |
| [`requirements.md`](requirements.md) | **Results:** functional and non-functional requirements, each traced back to the notes, recordings, and images above. |

## Method

1. **Listen to users (notes).** We collected public comments, reviews, and
   studies about how students search for scholarly evidence, and why they
   leave library databases for Google or AI tools.
2. **Watch users (recordings).** We reviewed videos of people using the
   current GMU library tools and competing AI research assistants, and
   noted every manual step and point of friction.
3. **Show the product (images).** We drew the current workflow and sketched
   wireframes of Evidence Engine based on those findings.
4. **Write the requirements.** We turned the findings into requirements with
   IDs that trace back to their evidence.

## Key findings

- Students struggle to **choose keywords and build Boolean queries**.
- They **prefer Google's ease of use** but **trust library sources more**.
- **Getting to the full text** through library links is confusing.
- AI tools often see **only abstracts**, and their summaries can **miss or
  misstate the main finding**.
- Users want **exact quotes they can check**, plus **saving and export**.
