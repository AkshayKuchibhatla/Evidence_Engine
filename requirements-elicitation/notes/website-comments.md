# Notes: Comments from Websites

We read comments, reviews, and studies on public websites to learn how students
search for scholarly evidence today and what frustrates them. Each note gives
the source, what it says (quoted or closely paraphrased), and what it suggests
for Evidence Engine.

> Sources were collected in October 2026. Review-site claims, especially about
> prices, change often. Check them again before you rely on them.

---

## A. Students searching library databases (the current way)

| # | Source | What it says | What it means for us |
|---|--------|--------------|----------------------|
| A1 | UC Merced library assessment poster ([PDF](https://assessment.ucmerced.edu/sites/assessment.ucmerced.edu/files/page/documents/scholarly_sources_library_poster_spring2014.pdf)) | Students had **difficulty finding suitable keywords** for databases and ended up with **too many or too few results**. | Users should be able to type a plain-language thesis. The tool turns it into keyword or Boolean queries. |
| A2 | Same UC Merced assessment | Students **default to Google**, which has little scholarly content. | The tool must be at least as easy to use as Google. |
| A3 | Same UC Merced assessment | **Research takes longer than expected.** | The main goal is to save time getting from thesis to usable quotes. |
| A4 | Same UC Merced assessment | Students were **unsure how to get to the full text** through library links. | Each result links straight to the full text through GMU access. |
| A5 | Inside Higher Ed, *Library Babel Fish* blog ([link](https://www.insidehighered.com/blogs/library-babel-fish/indirections-find-directions-out)) | Librarians see students trying to **learn enough of the lingo to come up with useful search terms** and then sort through possible sources. | Suggest discipline-specific terms and rank the sources for the user. |
| A6 | Minnesota Daily opinion piece ([link](https://mndaily.com/opinion/opinion-the-umn-libraries-online-resources-are-underrated/11/22/2025/eicmndaily-com/)) | Many students are **hesitant toward library databases** because they **don't know what's available**. | Search several GMU databases at once so the student doesn't need to pick one. |
| A7 | *Undergraduates' Use of Google vs. Library Resources: A Four-Year Study*, College & Research Libraries ([PDF](https://crl.acrl.org/index.php/crl/article/viewFile/16540/17986)) | After database training, many undergraduates **quickly go back to Google-dominant searching**, sometimes during the training session itself. | Training doesn't stick, so the product has to be simple enough that it needs none. |
| A8 | *Is "Good Enough" OK?*, University of Kansas ([PDF](https://kuscholarworks.ku.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/97b04ecc-8e34-4c86-9cfe-ad58398da9d6/content)) | Students **preferred the ease and intuitive interfaces of web search engines** but **valued the credible information in library databases**. | Pair a Google-style interface with library-quality sources. |
| A9 | Alotaibi et al. 2023, *Google Scholar or University Digital Libraries* ([SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/09610006221111197)) | Google Scholar scored higher on **ease of use and satisfaction**. Library systems scored higher on **comprehensiveness**. | Same conclusion as A8: keep the library's coverage and make it easy to use. |
| A10 | *Beyond Google Scholar* tutorial ([link](https://oerhub.tesshub.hzdr.de/materials/beyond-google-scholar)) | Students **often struggle to find relevant literature for thesis projects**. | Confirms the core problem Evidence Engine targets. |

## B. Users of similar AI research tools (Elicit, Consensus)

| # | Source | What it says | What it means for us |
|---|--------|--------------|----------------------|
| B1 | Elicit review, *toolsforhumans.ai* ([link](https://www.toolsforhumans.ai/ai-tools/elicit)) and other review sites | **PDF upload is paywalled**, and exporting to Excel or CSV needs a paid plan. | Users want to export. We should offer free export of quotes and citations (CSV, BibTeX, APA/MLA). |
| B2 | Elicit reviews (several, e.g. [unite.ai](https://www.unite.ai/elicit-ai-review/)) | When a paper is behind a strict paywall, Elicit **can often only read the abstract**. | This is our edge. GMU students have licensed full-text access, so we can quote from the body of the paper. |
| B3 | Elicit review ([manusights.com](https://manusights.com/blog/is-elicit-worth-it)) | AI can **misread tables, miss qualifying language, or attach a citation that is related but doesn't support the exact claim**. | Show each quote word for word with its page number, so the student can check it. Never paraphrase a quote. |
| B4 | Commenter on Eric Hudson's *Substack* review ([link](https://erichudson.substack.com/p/using-ai-for-search-and-research/comments)) | One-sentence summaries sometimes **focused on background information instead of the main finding**. | Rank quotes by how directly they support the thesis, not by where they appear in the paper. |
| B5 | Elicit reviews (same as above) | It **occasionally misses highly relevant papers** and **struggles with niche topics**. | Search several GMU databases (ProQuest, JSTOR, EBSCO, and others), not one index. |
| B6 | Consensus review, *unite.ai* ([link](https://www.unite.ai/consensus-ai-review/)) | Users like results that come with **summaries, quality indicators, citation counts, and links** to each study. | Show peer-review status, citation count, and year on every result. |
| B7 | Consensus review, *unite.ai* | Open-ended questions, or questions that **need logical reasoning**, don't always work. | A thesis is usually an argument, not a yes/no question. Our matching has to handle claims, not only questions. |
| B8 | University of Central Arkansas research compliance page on Consensus ([link](https://uca.edu/researchcompliance/consensus/)) | **"AI outputs are not evidence; the papers are."** Students should check the original paper. | Always show the source quote, with one click through to the paper. |
| B9 | Consensus on Product Hunt ([link](https://www.producthunt.com/products/consensus-2/reviews)) | Users like **fast science-based answers**. One user **wanted a way to store and retrieve findings**. | Add saved projects: keep a thesis with its collected quotes. |
| B10 | Consensus and Elicit review sites | Free tiers are limited (for example, a small number of AI credits each month). | Students on a budget want a free tool. Running it through GMU library access keeps it free for them. |

---

## Themes from the comments

1. **Search terms are hard.** Students don't know the database vocabulary (A1, A5).
2. **Ease of use beats quality.** Students go back to Google even though they trust library sources more (A2, A7, A8, A9).
3. **Full-text access is confusing** (A4), and AI tools often see only abstracts (B2).
4. **Trust has to be checkable.** Users want exact quotes, page numbers, and links, not AI paraphrases (B3, B4, B8).
5. **Students want to save and export** their findings (B1, B9).
6. **Time is the main cost.** Research takes longer than students expect (A3).
